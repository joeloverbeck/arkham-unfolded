# Restricted action marker presentation

Research on 2026-11-01 against `6e1bcac3febd4dcd6d15ed93cb4d8c8e42a4fbdc`, supporting the clue/resource/action-counter design interview. Joel subsequently accepted the marker design and bounded local implementation under [#73](https://github.com/joeloverbeck/arkham-unfolded/issues/73), then accepted the resulting refinement on 2026-11-01 after playing the complete prepared Party attempt through Act advancement and checking the recent visuals. This note preserves the inspected code, printed-card evidence and design reasoning; the [ADR refinement](../adr/0002-persistent-table-areas.md#clue-resource-and-action-counters) records the selected scope and acceptance. The research phase changed no runtime, assets, records or archive contents. The reported owner run does not establish a separate Tony or Jacqueline playthrough.

## Selected design

Keep three ordinary class disks together. Place Tony's additional Rogue disk after a small gap, with a raised **+** badge on its rim and a permanent two-line caption: **Fight / Engage** and **Enemy with bounty**. The gap and badge distinguish its restricted allowance without depending on color. Center the complete row, including the gap and additional marker, below the resource/clue row. The badge is an application cue, not an official Arkham symbol. The final placement and owner acceptance are recorded in the ADR refinement.

Retain the class face instead of replacing it with the combat fist. A fist suggests Fight but does not express Engage, the bounty condition, or that this is an additional action. A bounty symbol alone can be mistaken for the bounty quantity. No inspected single pictogram conveys all three restrictions. The permanent caption explains them without a hover dependency; full inspection of Tony remains the authoritative card-reading path.

The marker is a display of the supplied action state, not a new action button or a choice of which allowance to spend. Its cross changes only when the rules authority spends that allowance. The existing canceled-chaos-token cross provides reusable visual geometry, but the action marker means **spent**, not canceled. ## Current implementation and edge cases

Tony supplies separate `ordinary_actions` and `bonus_action` values. At the researched baseline his summary explicitly named the extra Fight/Engage allowance, and his payment path consumed it on a qualifying action before ordinary actions. The implemented counter presentation preserves that distinction. [Observation and payment](../../src/tony.rs), [tabletop presentation](../../src/tony_tabletop.rs)

Selected visual semantics:

- During an active turn, uncrossed ordinary markers indicate remaining allowance. A marker alone does not assert that every action is legal; existing choices govern legality.
- Tony's bonus remains uncrossed while unused. At an action boundary, if no eligible enemy/action exists, subdue it while retaining the permanent Fight/Engage and bounty restriction; do not cross it as spent. Derive this from the supplied legal choices and restriction, not an artwork heuristic. The Party guide explains its lack of eligible bounty enemies. A pending test or other decision can temporarily prevent action selection without removing its target; the marker must not mislabel that interval as target absence.
- On early turn end, subdue all remaining uncrossed markers. Keep actual spent crosses and the existing named turn-ended indication. Do not rewrite unspent actions as spent merely to gray the row. Before a turn starts, uncrossed markers likewise do not imply that the investigator may interrupt another turn.
- Replay displays the recorded moment's allowance and turn state. Opening a record, moving the camera or rebuilding the scene cannot spend or refresh an action. The bounded experiments do not support a following round, so do not invent a reset transition there.

Party supplies ordinary action counts and `turn_finished`; ending early preserves the unused count. Its enclosing observation also supplies `tony_bonus_action`, fixed as unused in this bounded experiment, which has no eligible enemy action. Show that supplied additional allowance as unused but unavailable rather than omitting it or inventing an enemy action. Jacqueline's older test experiment does not represent action allowances at all. Use the shared presentation where the consumer supplies the facts, while keeping these experiment boundaries honest. ## Strong alternative and remaining evidence

A separate small **Bonus action** plaque carrying the same restriction text is the strongest simpler alternative. It is immediately explicit and avoids crowding four disks under a narrow investigator card, but breaks the requested physical-token row and duplicates a second visual vocabulary. Prefer the separated disk if the actual focused view keeps its two-line caption readable; use the plaque if native inspection shows that the disk or caption crowds adjacent regions. An icon-only fourth disk is smaller but loses essential meaning and is not recommended.

The archive also supplies white/transparent Fight and Engage artwork at `images/bundles/7af1e2752099652f0a9dbc894013fd3c9803ec75e44885106e4c613b59763445/Texture2D/Fight--ea987555c580c15c.png` and `Engage--07bdde63109041dc.png` in that same directory. Pairing these on the additional disk is a plausible alternative to the class face, but it still needs the bounty restriction in words and adds two small symbols to an already narrow marker. The inspected `images/other/unclassified/en/sce-base-save/Bounty--ImageURL--5e10a5f17dda3cb8d795.png` is a detailed wanted-poster/crosshair design with a gold star border. Using it as a third miniature badge risks clutter and confusion with an actual bounty counter. Prefer the class face plus simple badge and caption; archive availability is not evidence of native readability or an official action-marker convention.

This selected design follows the existing local principles of [readable component controls](tabletop-contextual-controls.md#recommendation) and [visible consequence wording](attack-opportunity-and-harm-presentation.md#recommendation). Its rationale is a design inference, not comparative usability evidence. The verification surface covers an ordinary spend, a bonus spend with all three ordinary actions intact, no eligible target, an early-ended turn, missing artwork and recorded replay. Technical results and the owner's reported Party playthrough remain distinct evidence in #73 and the ADR refinement; acceptance of this bounded refinement does not establish full-game fitness or independent owner runs through the other experiments.
