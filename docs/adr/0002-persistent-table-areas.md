# Persistent table areas with focused camera views

Joel selected this presentation direction and the first implementation scope on 2026-10-29 during the `grill-with-docs` interview related to [#22](https://github.com/joeloverbeck/arkham-unfolded/issues/22), against the implementation at `4254f50d228399379cfeccf6917d5cf5fa7a0cfb`. This records the agreed design for the implementation handoff. #22 continues to own its existing prepared-turn work and separate gameplay acceptance; selecting this design does not close that issue or establish native usability.

## Decision

One persistent 3D scene contains a shared scenario surface and separate investigator surfaces in a centered row nearer the viewer. “Below” describes their arrangement in the default view, with the surfaces at the same height. Center the group for the participating investigators without shrinking cards to fit more areas. Establish positions at setup and keep them stable during play. Distinct surfaces sit close enough together to avoid wasting navigation space on gaps.

The scenario area contains the map, investigator standees, victory display, encounter deck and discard pile, and act and agenda cards. The investigator areas give Tony, Patrice and Jacqueline stable personal homes. Moving the camera never moves their cards into a replacement investigator dock.

A persistent side strip uses investigator portraits to focus their respective areas and provides access to the scenario area and an overview. An investigator focus gives that area most of the available scene view; the map may leave the view. During ordinary navigation and contextual action selection, a compact screen display retains the current decision, the investigator answering it, and any ongoing test. Deliberately opening full card inspection may temporarily cover this display. Inspecting or focusing another investigator does not change the turn or the decision's actor. Live camera control remains manual by default, consistent with the play contract.

Each investigator's hand remains physically displayed along the near edge of their own surface, separate from assets in play. Keep card faces individually visible at a consistent size; use additional rows and panning when crowded rather than shrinking or overlapping them. Joel accepted the resulting navigation trade-off: examining a hand normally means focusing its investigator area, rather than using a screen-fixed hand strip whose contents change with the selected investigator. Ordinary hands should fit comfortably in the focused view; a representative crowded position from the actual decks is still needed before claiming full-play fit.

One shared physical test area near the investigator-facing edge of the scenario surface displays committed cards and revealed/canceled chaos tokens. A Test focus shortcut provides quick access; the compact actor/test summary remains available elsewhere on the table. Tests keep this stable home rather than moving their staging between investigator areas. The camera stays under manual control by default. This is a presentation arrangement and does not add a new rules-defined zone.

Selecting a physical card or component is the normal entry point for its currently legal actions, with an explicit full-inspection control. Selection alone never commits a game choice. The pending actor and prompt remain visible during action selection; choices without a useful physical source, such as ending the turn or declining an optional effect, remain in the decision controls.

Inspect in the contextual controls opens the existing card inspection modal. Joel reports that this modal works well and selected retaining it instead of the proposed nonmodal edge panel. The modal continues to provide artwork and readable rules with its existing Close/Esc behavior. Opening or closing inspection does not choose a game action, change the camera, or discard a pending game decision or presentation-only selection. The contextual action menu closes when inspection opens; returning from inspection must not dispatch a stale action. Routine actions remain available through contextual controls without first opening full inspection. The benefit of a replacement inspector was unproven, so no inspector redesign is selected.

Following the [contextual-control research](../research/tabletop-contextual-controls.md), Joel selected small labeled radial controls for short action sets, with a compact nearby list for detailed wording or constrained space. Left-click selects the exact component; a separate click activates an available action. Costs and opportunity-attack warnings remain visible locally before activation. Recurring actions retain their positions within the same context as legality changes. Compound card or token selections retain their complete legal alternatives and explicit completion step. Labels remain upright and readable, menus stay within the viewport, right-drag continues to orbit, and menu keyboard navigation must not also move the camera. A revision or mode change invalidates transient choices; replay permits inspection only. Escape or clicking empty space dismisses the menu without a game action. The native usability of this selected design remains to be evaluated.

Manual navigation includes holding unmodified WASD keys to pan across the table plane relative to the current camera direction: W forward, S backward, A left and D right. Panning preserves camera tilt and zoom, and releasing the keys stops movement. Like manual orbit and zoom, it overrides automatic following. Camera keys must not act while another control captures keyboard input or a blocking modal is open. The existing orbit and zoom controls remain available. Neither experiment provided manual panning at the decision's baseline revision.

The overview supports orientation; focused views support readable play. Simultaneous readability of every card across the whole scene is not the aim. A permanent map inset or split view was considered but would reduce the space available to the focused investigator. The exact framing, spacing and transition timing remain reversible presentation choices to evaluate in use.

## Why

The current scene mixes a location, an enemy, an asset and a threat in one fixed row. Stable areas should make their different relationships easier to understand while preserving the physical tabletop experience. The owner's supplied layout study usefully distinguishes hands, assets, threats and shared collections, but its selected-investigator dock is not the chosen spatial model. Automating bookkeeping does not remove the desired physical presence of the components.

The principal risk is repeated camera travel during one decision. The existing Jacqueline/Patrice case spans a contributed card, Jacqueline's ability and shared chaos tokens; it is a concrete case for checking whether the persistent decision display preserves enough context. This is an evaluation candidate, not evidence that the new layout already works.

## Layout details

Investigator surfaces use class colors, with a muted tint and matching border and portrait accent. The six presentation schemes are Guardian blue, Seeker yellow/orange, Rogue green, Mystic purple, Survivor red and Neutral gray/white. The current investigators are Tony (Rogue), Patrice (Survivor) and Jacqueline (Mystic). Tony's secondary Guardian deckbuilding choice does not replace his Rogue identity. Exact shades and material treatment are implementation choices to judge in the visual result.

Following the first native candidate, Joel explicitly requested retaining the original rounded standee base and rectangular holder as the application's standard, including both experiments and subsequent scenario presentation. Both scenes now use the same renderer, with class-colored base layers and holder matching the investigator surface palette. This is a shared physical presentation component, not additional game-state support. Loaded standee pictures serve as the investigator focus buttons without adjacent names; unavailable pictures retain readable name buttons, and image buttons retain accessible names.

The starting internal arrangement puts threats along the far edge toward the scenario, the investigator card and personal status at one side, flexible asset rows centrally, deck and discard together at the other side, and the hand arrangement at the near edge. Assets use flexible rows rather than fixed equipment-slot boxes. Keep threats conspicuous as other regions fill. Bounty Contracts, Internal Injury and the Ghoul provide current examples for judging these groupings. Exact coordinates, spacing and available room for additional rows remain reversible implementation choices to inspect against real positions.

The research note preserves the evidence, limits and simpler local-list comparison behind the selected contextual controls. The modal inspector remains the existing implementation. Utility-control placement, menu dimensions, row spacing and responsive breakpoints are delegated implementation choices, not additional owner decisions.
